How does anyone know anything? We collect experiences and moments, little impressions of the world that inform our thoughts and future actions. These small artifacts and lessons we accumulate throughout our lives coalesce to form the lens through which we see our reality. The words we learn from our mothers become the language we utilize to shape and explore the world around us. Billions of neurons work together to form the machinery that performs the computations that give us our sense of reality, our ability to communicate, and our ability to think.
How do you know your perception is true? You put it to the test. Statistics- a field all about measuring, quantifying, and modeling the world around us- gives us a rigorous framework to test our intuition. It gives structure to our perception of the world, allowing us to distinguish the signal of truth from the noise of coincidence.
How do we discover something new? We collect data, whether through careful experimentation or by observing the world around us. But data is ugly. Data is boring. Data is frustrating. Data by itself doesn’t reveal or illuminate anything about the world. To make knowledge accessible, we must do more than just collect it; we must curate it. We must transform complex datasets into visual, compelling narratives that allow others to see the patterns we have uncovered.

This portfolio tells a story of data. A story of exploring human cognition through data- both to inform and persuade. As a neuroscientist I frequently work with faceted, high-dimensional datasets and models to map the brain. As a science communicator I seek to transform this data into stories that are accessible to all, regardless of their background.
